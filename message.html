A Word To Ponder

Brethren, Are We Truly Living Out Our Calling?

A reflection on faith, service, and what it means to truly follow Christ beyond the four walls of the church.

Sunday service, worship service, prayer service, mission service — so many church services take place every week. But have we ever stopped to ask ourselves: have these services become mere rituals in our Christian lives, just like they may have become for you and me?

What is God calling us to do today?

Are we simply attending one service after another, listening to powerful speakers, enjoying beautiful worship, and giving our weekly offerings to the church — and then going home, doing nothing, and waiting for the last day to come?

Is this the kind of life our Lord Jesus Christ has called us to live?

Are we feeding the physical church, making it bigger and bigger, building impressive facilities, and developing wonderful worship teams, while so many souls outside the church have yet to hear the Gospel and experience the love of Jesus?

Brethren, what is our role, our responsibility, and our calling as those who have believed in Christ, received baptism, and been called to live as children of God? Are we truly faithful to our Lord Jesus Christ if we simply attend church every week but never step out to reach the lost, serve the needy, or share the love of God with others?

John 3:16 reminds us of God's great love for the world. Jesus did not come merely to establish physical gatherings or to give us weekly religious activities. He came to save us from sin and bring us into eternal life through Him.

There is a difference between attending church and becoming a true disciple of Jesus Christ. There is a difference between hearing the Word and living out the Word. There is a difference between singing about God's love and allowing His love to flow through us to others.

Brethren, where is our heart today? Is it centred only on ourselves, our own spiritual comfort, our positions, our activities, and the things that benefit us? Or are our hearts aligned with the heart of our Lord Jesus Christ?

Please, let us pray and allow the Holy Spirit to awaken us. Let us examine our hearts honestly before the Lord.

Jesus came to seek and to save the lost. He came to restore lives, reconcile people to God, and offer eternal life to all who believe in Him. His sacrifice was not for us to become comfortable Christians who simply sit back and watch the world go by. We are saved by grace, and we are called to live out that grace by loving God and loving others.

Remember the parable of the lost sheep. The shepherd leaves the ninety-nine to seek the one that is lost (Luke 15:3–7). Why? Because that one matters to him. He cares. He loves. He desires to bring the lost one home.

Brethren, do we share the heart of our Good Shepherd?

Our mission is not simply to fill more seats in physical buildings, organise more events, or build impressive worship teams. These things can serve God's purposes, but they must never replace our calling to make disciples, care for people, and bring the Gospel to those who have yet to know Jesus.

Let us not measure our faithfulness merely by how many services we attend, how many sermons we hear, or how much we give. Let us also examine the fruit of our lives: whom have we loved, whom have we served, whom have we encouraged, and whom have we helped to come closer to Jesus?

Brethren, the harvest is truly plenteous, but the labourers are few (Matthew 9:37–38).

May the Holy Spirit awaken our hearts. May we no longer be Christians who only receive, but disciples who are willing to go. May we not merely gather within the four walls of the church, but also step outside to reach those who are hurting, forgotten, lonely, poor, and lost.

We are saved to serve. We are loved to love. We are called to follow Jesus and to make disciples.

May our lives become a living testimony of the love and salvation of our Lord Jesus Christ — not only on Sundays, but every day, wherever He leads us.

All glory to our Lord Jesus Christ. Amen.
